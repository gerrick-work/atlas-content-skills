# Atlas script lab

The writing brain for Atlas content. `explainer-reel` (Stage 2) and `atlas-social-posts` (Copy) load this skill before they write a word, then go back to their own steps for production. On its own, it answers "give me hooks for X", "sharpen this script" or "what should the CTA be".

It does not design, render or publish. It returns one **angle brief** (section 10) that the calling skill builds from.

Method adapted from the MIT-licensed coreyhaines31/marketingskills library (hook system, awareness-stage headlines, offer anatomy, carousel frameworks, AI-tells), rewritten for Atlas.

## 1. Inputs

Take these from the calling skill, the Odoo task (project "Atlas Content Calendar", id 5) or the request. Ask once, in one AskUserQuestion, only for what is missing.

- **Topic** and any angle already written on the task
- **Account:** Atlas DSF (B2B: brand owners, founders, SMEs) or Atlas Origins (B2C: fragrance lovers, beginners, makers)
- **Format:** reel, carousel or static
- **Platform register:** Instagram/Facebook by default; TikTok is simpler and more relatable ("kilang perfume" framing is fine) but never cheap; LinkedIn (DSF captions) is more measured
- **Funnel role:** Discover, Trust or Convert (from the task's TOFU/MOFU/BOFU tag: TOFU = Discover, MOFU = Trust, BOFU = Convert). If missing, infer it and say so.

## 2. Ground it first

Read these memory files: `atlas-positioning`, `the-bench`, `atlas-scaling`, `social-accounts`, plus `inbound-lead-gating` and `organic-content-funnel` for Convert pieces.

Rules that carry into every line:
- Messaging order for DSF: product development and manufacturing first, fragrance second, AI third. Every DSF piece supports "Atlas helps brands turn product ideas into finished products."
- Atlas owns the formula; clients get exclusive use, never ownership. Never write "your formula" as if they own it.
- Only claims Atlas can defend today. No locations, invented figures, client names, prices or third parties unless Gerrick gives them. Never expand "DSF".
- Content must not reveal stock, suppliers or tools.
- About 80% mass-market and simply explained, 20% niche.
- Gerrick wants reels to stimulate: provocative, surprising or visually striking, never gentle tips. Section 6b is the test.

## 3. Language research (5 minutes, 2 to 3 searches)

Hooks written in marketing voice lose to hooks written in the buyer's own words. Before writing, read the research notes on the Odoo task (they often hold the top comment themes and thread titles), then run 2 to 3 WebSearch queries for how real people talk about the topic, for example:
- DSF: Malaysian founders asking how to start a perfume brand, find an OEM manufacturer, or why private-label scents smell the same (Lowyat, Reddit r/smallbusiness or r/fragrance, Facebook seller groups, TikTok comments)
- Origins: what beginners ask about notes, longevity, layering, dupes, making their own

Write 3 to 6 verbatim phrases into the brief with their source. Take the vocabulary only, never a creator's specific idea or wording. If nothing useful turns up, say so and write from the segment table below.

## 4. Pick the reader and the stage

**Segment.** One specific slice of the audience, never "brands" or "everyone". Starting hypotheses (replace them as real comments and DMs come in):

| Account | Segment | Motivations to draw from |
|---|---|---|
| DSF | First-time founder with a brand idea and no manufacturer | doesn't know where to start; fears a big minimum order; fears being scammed |
| DSF | Existing brand selling a stock or "pati" scent | smells like competitors; stuck in a price war; customers don't come back |
| DSF | Lifestyle SME adding a fragrance product (cafe, salon, hotel, retailer) | wants a signature scent; no technical team; worries about compliance |
| DSF | Corporate gifting buyer | needs something memorable on a deadline; needs it to look premium |
| Origins | Fragrance lover / collector | wants to understand why a scent works; dupes; longevity |
| Origins | Beginner maker / hobbyist | where to start; which materials; why their blend smells flat |

**Awareness stage sets the angle and the CTA:**

| Stage | What the viewer needs | Angle that works | Funnel role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | to recognise a situation they're in | a feeling or a specific moment | Discover |
| Problem-aware | a credible way out of a named problem | pain plus outcome; the hidden cause | Discover / Trust |
| Solution-aware | why this approach differs | how it actually works; before and after | Trust |
| Product-aware | an objection answered with proof | proof, process, scope, what you get | Convert |

**Discover pieces can start from the shopper's side.** A mass-market viewer (a shopper, a fan) is a bigger audience than a founder. Open on the question a shopper asks, then flip the viewer into the brand owner's seat at the pattern break (section 6b). This reaches both and still lands the B2B point.

## 5. Hooks: a matrix, not a list

A hook is three parts working together. Write all three every time:

| Part | Reel | Carousel / static |
|---|---|---|
| Visual | what is on screen in the first 2 seconds; something must be happening (cutting, splitting, pouring, breaking), never a still product | the cover image or cut-out |
| Spoken | the first voice line, under 2 seconds | (none) |
| On-screen | the text anchor | the cover headline |

**No duplication.** The parts must add to each other, not repeat. If the voice asks "Why is perfume so expensive?", the screen answers sideways ("You're paying for the shelf.") and the visual shows scissors cutting a price tag. On a static, the headline must not caption the image.

**Generate 6 to 8 hooks across different cells** (segment x motivation x opening move). Ten rewordings of one idea teach nothing. Opening moves to rotate through:

| Move | Shape | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer's question | the exact question they type or ask, from section 3: "Why is perfume so expensive?" | use their phrasing; answer it within 3 seconds |
| Curiosity gap | withhold the key noun: "The ingredient that decides if your perfume lasts" | must pay off inside the piece |
| Provocation | challenge a common belief or practice | state the correct fact directly; keep it fair |
| Specific bold claim | falsifiable and defensible: "You can launch a perfume with 500 bottles" | needs proof on screen |
| Proof-first | open on the result: the finished bottle, the doc set, the number | strongest when the proof needs no explaining |
| Specific POV | mirror one exact situation: "Your supplier just asked for 3,000 units upfront" | generic POV is invisible |
| Three beats and a turn | "AI can see. It can hear. It still can't smell." | the turn must be the topic |
| Countdown / list | "3 things your manufacturer won't tell you" | the count must be real |

**Banned openers** (they read as gentle tips and lose the scroll): "Your [product] sells for...", "Did you know...", "Here's why...", "Let's talk about...", "Today we...", "If you're a founder...", and any line that sets up a scenario before the tension. The tension or the question comes in the first five words.

**Score each hook 1 to 3** on: specific (names a real thing), true (defensible from section 2), buyer's language (from section 3), pays off (the body delivers it), fits the stage, and **stops the scroll** (would a stranger with no interest in perfume brands still watch second 3?). Score out of 18. Pick the highest as the lead and keep the next two as **alternates**: ready-made variants for testing when the post performs (see `meta-ad-iteration` memory).

## 6. The on-ramp (seconds 2 to 10, or slide 2)

The beat after the hook must continue the hook's premise and pay it off fast, never pivot to "At Atlas, we...". If the hook asks why, the next line gives the blunt answer. If the hook promises the cause of a problem, the next line starts explaining the cause. Introduce Atlas only once the viewer has the idea.

## 6b. Stimulation check (reels must pass all four)

1. **Open loop or fight in the first 2 seconds.** A question the viewer wants answered, or a claim they want to argue with.
2. **Blunt answer by second 5.** A number or a short hard sentence ("Because the shop can keep half of what you paid.").
3. **Pattern break near the middle.** A line that changes the viewer's seat or the energy ("Now flip it. You're the brand."). The calling skill changes the visual register there too.
4. **A stakes line before the payoff.** What goes wrong if they ignore this, in concrete words ("your margin dies the day a retailer calls"). Strong verbs, no hedging adjectives, and still defensible.

If any of the four is missing, rewrite before returning the brief.

## 7. The meat

**Reel spine (about 100 to 120 words, 35 to 50s):**
1. Hook (0 to 2s): three parts from section 5
2. On-ramp: the blunt answer, one or two lines
3. Mechanism: how it actually works, 2 to 3 lines, one idea per line, one concrete example
4. Pattern break (section 6b)
5. Stakes line (section 6b)
6. Proof: one defensible fact, number, document or process step from the claims ledger
7. Payoff: what to do instead, and Atlas's role in one line
8. CTA: section 8, spoken and on screen

**Carousel frameworks (pick the one that fits the content):**

| Framework | Use when | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Value-stack | a set of tips, materials, steps | cover states the exact count and payoff; one item per slide; CTA |
| Problem-proof | Atlas did something and can show it | result as the hook; the real problem; the process; the receipt (doc, bottle, test) |
| Myth fix | a common belief is wrong | the belief; why people hold it; the correct fact with evidence; what to do instead |
| Process walkthrough | showing how something gets made | numbered stages with a cut-out per stage; what the client does vs what Atlas does |
| Before / after | two states compare cleanly | before; what changed; after; how |

Slide 1 must work alone in the feed. Every body slide holds one idea and earns the next swipe.

**Make it concrete.** Name the material, the document (IFRA certificate, SDS, COA), the step, the quantity. Replace every vague phrase ("high quality", "premium", "seamless") with the specific thing it stands for, or cut it.

## 8. CTA and offer

**One CTA per piece, matched to the funnel role:**

| Role | CTA type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | low-effort engagement that builds the retargeting audience | "Save this for your next launch", "Follow for how it's made", "Send this to the friend starting a brand", "Did you know the shop keeps this much? Tell us below." |
| Trust | keyword capture | "Comment LAUNCH and we'll send you the [live lead magnet]", "DM us SAMPLE" |
| Convert | direct next step | "Message us on WhatsApp to start your brief" (link in bio), the quiz when it exists |

**Only point at offers that exist.** Before using a keyword CTA, check the Active offers table below. If the lead magnet isn't live, use a DM keyword that Gerrick answers by hand, or drop to a Discover CTA, and flag it in the brief.

| Offer | Status | Keyword | Funnel role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perfume launch cost calculator (lead magnet) | planned, not live | (set when live) | Trust |
| Fragrance launch checklist (lead magnet) | proposed, not live | LAUNCH | Trust |
| Concept-to-shelf package | under consideration; confirm scope, price and scarcity with Gerrick before stating any | (none) | Convert |

Gerrick updates this table as offers go live.

**Building a Convert offer line.** Frame it as an outcome, not a feature list ("a finished, shelf-ready product with its full document set", not "formulation + filling + packaging"). State scope plainly. Add extras only where each one closes a real objection (first-sample rejection: a revision round; compliance fear: the IFRA, SDS, COA and TDS set). Urgency and scarcity only when real and confirmed. No price unless Gerrick gives it.

**Reels:** the CTA is spoken as the last voice line and shown on the end card. **Captions:** the first line repeats the hook idea in different words (not the same sentence), the CTA appears again before the hashtags.

## 9. Edit pass: cut the AI tells

Read every line once more and fix:
- **"Not X. Y." reveals** and their variants ("It's not X, it's Y", "Less X, more Y"): state Y directly with its reason.
- **Negation lists** ("No X, no Y, just Z"): say what does happen.
- **Trailing pile-ons:** a finished claim followed by a comma and more benefits. End the sentence.
- **Self-answered questions** ("The result?", "The catch?") and **colon reveals** ("The secret: consistency"): write a normal sentence. The opening hook question is the one exception, because the next line answers it outright.
- **Filler vocabulary:** unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionary, delve, journey, landscape, curated, effortless.
- **Soft verbs:** swap "can affect", "may impact", "helps with" for the concrete outcome ("dies", "breaks", "keeps half") wherever the claim supports it.
- **Em dashes:** none, anywhere. Rewrite the sentence.
- Every line passes: "Could only Atlas have written this?" If a competitor could post it unchanged, add the specific detail.
